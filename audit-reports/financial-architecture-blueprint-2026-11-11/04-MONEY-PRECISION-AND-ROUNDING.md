# 04 — Money precision and rounding contract (PROPOSED)

Use checked integer minor units only where currency exponent and range are known; otherwise use decimal strings at API boundaries and PostgreSQL NUMERIC for calculation. Proposed intermediate storage is NUMERIC(24,8), subject to bounds review. Go uses currency-tagged decimal/checked minor units. JavaScript uses decimal strings or BigInt minor units; never binary floating point for money. JSON amounts are canonical decimal strings with ISO currency and exponent metadata. Currency exponents are data, not an assumption of two digits.

Version calculation rules and retain source precision, rule version and quantized amount. Tax-exclusive and tax-inclusive calculations need explicit quantization level. Allocate discounts in minor units proportionally using largest remainder with stable line-ID tie break. Split tenders sum exactly to payable. Refund allocations cannot exceed captured less previous refunds. FX records original amount/currency, rate/source/time and functional amount; gains/losses are explicit entries. Negative refunds/reversals retain signed source evidence but journal debit/credit magnitudes remain positive.

Illustration only: three net lines of 10.00 receive a 1.00 discount, allocated 0.34/0.33/0.33. Net total is 29.00; at an illustrative 10% tax-exclusive rate, invoice-level tax rounds to 2.90. Per-line tax rounds 0.97 + 0.97 + 0.97 = 2.91. The 0.01 difference shows why policy must choose line-level or invoice-level rounding; no rate is implied. A final fixture must be independently calculated under the selected method. Currency list, limits, tax method, rounding mode/tolerance and FX source are unresolved accountant/owner decisions.
